Rows move for real620ms · FLIP

When XP changes the list re-sorts and every row slides to its new position rather than snapping. Measure each row's position, re-order, apply the inverse transform, release. Standard FLIP — about fifteen lines and it is the single thing that makes a leaderboard feel alive instead of printed.

Being passed is an eventtoast + 900ms flash

If somebody overtakes you, their row and yours both flash a white inset border and a toast names them. Getting passed should sting slightly. That sting is the entire motivational engine of a leaderboard.

Your row is invertedwhite on black

The one place the system spends its single accent. You can find yourself in a list of 180 without reading a word, and it holds wherever you sit.

Bars behind the rows900ms fill

Each row carries a fill proportional to the leader's score. It turns a column of numbers into a shape, which is how people actually read standings.

Reset countdown1s tick

Days plus a ticking clock. A monthly board with no visible clock is just a ranking; with one it is a deadline. Urgency climbs by itself in the last week.

The gap linerecalculates live

“45 XP off sixth — one Reel”. Never show a raw gap. Price it in a task they know how to do, and it updates every time anything moves.

Ambient brick fieldflash per event

Same field as every other screen. A brick flashes whenever anyone on the board scores, so peripheral vision registers activity before the list does.

Last month's winner22s slow push

A face and one line about how they did it. Keeps the previous cycle alive for a week and quietly tells everyone what winning actually took.

02What a leaderboard has to survive

Most leaderboards die for the same three reasons. Each has a fix, and all three fixes are on this screen.

FailureFix
The order stops changingMonthly reset. A lifetime board is won permanently by whoever joined first, and the day the order freezes is the day people stop opening it.
The bottom two-thirds disengageTop ten public, your own row pinned wherever you sit, and the gap priced in one task. Nobody has to read their position as hopeless.
It becomes a commission tableRank on work delivered, never on rupees. Sales-ranked boards expose earnings between athletes and make small-audience creators permanently invisible.
The one that does the most work

The overtake animation. A static leaderboard is a document; a leaderboard where you watched someone slide past you is a competition. It costs about fifteen lines of FLIP and it is the difference between a screen people check once a month and a screen they leave open.

03Rules for this screen

  1. Never animate a position change the athlete caused themselves without showing the number first. XP lands, the value flashes, then the row moves. Reversing that order reads as a glitch.
  2. Only notify on being passed, never on passing. Telling someone they overtook a rival invites gloating and makes the board feel like a fight. Being passed is private and motivating; passing should be something they notice themselves.
  3. Cap the live updates. Poll at most every thirty seconds in production, and batch anything that arrives together. A board that reshuffles every two seconds is noise, not life.
  4. Opted-out athletes vanish from the public ten but keep their own row. Per §1.10 of the field manual. Their position still counts; their handle just is not shown.
  5. Freeze the board for the final hour of the month. A position that changes at 23:58 on the 31st feels arbitrary. Announce the freeze and let the last hour be suspense rather than churn.
  6. The gap line is always one task away from true. If the nearest rival is 400 XP ahead, say so plainly rather than pricing it in nine Reels. An honest impossible gap is better than a dishonest achievable one.
