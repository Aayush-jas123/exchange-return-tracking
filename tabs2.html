Demo controls — not part of the app These fire events that would normally come from your console or a courier scan, so you can watch how the screens react. An athlete never sees them.

TaskTab 1 · the default

Opens on progress, not on work: how many tasks stand between them and the next level, which ones are cleared, how much XP that was worth. Scroll and the one timed task for today is underneath. The cue bar between them carries the countdown and jumps you to it.

ClimbTab 2 · where am I

Level, the gap, every unmet condition, the six rungs with cleared dates, and what the next one unlocks.

GamesTab 3 · a shell for now

The public wall is gone — athletes no longer see each other’s tasks or proof. In its place, four candidate game formats and the match room where chat would live. What games pay out is still open, and §05 argues for one answer over the others.

RanksTab 4 · the competition

Week, month and all time, each ranking only athletes at the same Comfort Level. Underneath, a breakdown of where their own XP actually came from. Leave the tab and come back — the board does not pause because you looked away.

LogTab 5 · the collection

Every discomfort task as a tile. The empty slots pull harder than the full ones.

And not a tab: the monthly menua push, not a destination

Tap the banner on Task. Pick-two-of-five happens once a month, so it is a card that appears and then goes away — not a permanent slot competing with screens people open daily.

The avatar, top rightfive screens, no tabs

Your code, earnings, shipments, the athlete card and settings. Each gets opened a handful of times a month, usually because a WhatsApp message sent them there. Putting any one of them in the bar would cost a daily screen its place.

02Signing in

Phone number, six-digit code on WhatsApp, ninety days. No password, because a password is a thing to forget, a reset flow to build and a support queue to staff — and you would have to build phone verification anyway, since that is the only honest way to reset one. Press Sign out under the phone to see the whole flow.

DetailWhy it is built this way
A wrong number looks identical to a right oneEnter a number that is not on the roster and you still get the code screen, with the same wording and the same delay. A login form that says “no such athlete” is a tool for walking your roster.
WhatsApp first, SMS as fallbackYour athletes are already in that thread, it costs less, and it is more reliable than Indian SMS routes. SMS is the backup, not the default.
Five attempts, then the code diesTry a wrong code in the demo and watch the counter. Six digits is a one-in-a-million guess only because the attempt limit makes it one — without it, it is a few hours of scripting.
Ninety-day sliding sessionEach visit pushes the expiry out, so an athlete who opens the app weekly never sees this screen again. The login is a doorway, not a turnstile.
Discharge logs them out instantlySessions are rows in your database, not self-contained tokens. Blacklisting someone deletes every row in the same transaction, and their next tap is this screen.
What this replaced

The earlier design gave every athlete a permanent private link with no login, plus four short-lived token links and a five-layer scheme to stop the permanent one leaking. All of it is gone. One login replaces five link types, and it buys something the links could never allow: an athlete can now see their own address, phone, order history and commission, none of which could ever render on a URL that might be forwarded.

The one thing worth keeping from the old design

A whitelist serialiser on every athlete-facing response. Decide explicitly what goes out rather than serialising the model, and no future developer can expose internal notes, another athlete's standing or a reviewer's identity by adding a column. Thirty lines, and it outlives every refactor.

03The rule that decides a tab

A tab is somewhere you go repeatedly and unprompted. If a screen is only ever reached because something sent you there, it is a push, not a tab.

Three things I changed from the earlier mockups

The Wall is gone and Games replaced it. Publishing what every athlete did exposed one person's tasks and proof to everyone on the roster, which is not a trade this program needs to make. Games keep the daily reason to return without putting anybody's work on display.

The leaderboard headline is now Standings. It used to say “The Board” while a different Board tab sat directly underneath it, which would have confused athletes inside a day.

The top bar carries identity, not a breadcrumb. It used to echo the screen name, which every screen already prints in its own headline — so the same words appeared twice, 10px apart. It now holds handle · current Comfort Level on every tab, which is the one thing an athlete should never have to go looking for. Wayfinding is the tab bar’s job.

On the tab-switch animation

240ms, a small rise and fade, and nothing else. Tab switches happen dozens of times a session, so this is the one place in the app where motion must get out of the way. Save the theatre for the promotion — see FM-OFC-04.

04The five behind the avatar

A sheet, not a sixth tab and not a separate page. It slides up over whatever the athlete was looking at, hugs its own content so the short screens stay short, and closes back to exactly where they were. Tapping a row pushes one level deep; the arrow comes back. That is the entire navigation model — two levels, no more.

ScreenOpened whenWhat it must never do
Your codeWriting a caption, answering a DMMake them hunt for the code. It is the only element at display size on the screen.
EarningsAround payout, once or twice a monthShow a figure the athlete cannot reconcile. Every deduction is a named line — self-purchase, refunds — so nobody has to ask why the number moved.
ShipmentsPushed — a dispatch message sent themMake them ask your team where a kit is. The tracker carries the AWB and the current leg.
Athlete cardAfter a promotion, when they want to post itCarry anything private. Level, handle, XP, rank — nothing else, because this image leaves the app.

Three details worth copying exactly

  • The sheet hugs its content. The account menu is five rows, so the sheet is short; Earnings after unlock is long, so it grows to 90% and scrolls inside. A fixed-height sheet would leave a third of the screen empty on the menu and feel broken.
  • The dot lives on the avatar, not inside it. Ship a kit using the control under the phone and a dot appears top right. It clears when they open Shipments — not when the sheet opens, and not on a timer. A badge that clears itself teaches people to ignore badges.
  • Logging in is what unlocks money, and nothing else is asked. There is no second code in front of Earnings. An athlete who holds a live session is the athlete, and making them prove it twice reads as distrust rather than security. The protection lives at the door — a 90-day session that dies the moment they are discharged, or the moment they sign out everywhere.
Why not a sixth tab

Five is already the practical ceiling for a bottom bar on a phone — a sixth tab makes every label too narrow to read at a glance and shrinks the tap targets below comfortable. More to the point, none of these five earns one. The test has not changed: open the app on a day with nothing due, and you will open Task, Climb, Games, Ranks and Log. You will not open Settings.

05Two things still open

What games pay out

This is the one that has to be answered before a line of game code is written, because it decides what the Comfort Levels mean.

Sales were routed to coins rather than XP for a specific reason: so a creator with eight hundred followers who does every brief can out-climb one with eighty thousand who sells well and skips the hard ones. Games break that logic harder than sales ever could. A sale at least requires somebody in the world to buy something; a game requires a free evening. If games award XP, the fastest route to Zero Comfort is a sofa.

OptionWhat it does to the ladder
Coins onlyGames are worth playing and worth winning, and the ladder still only moves on approved discomfort work. Same rule as sales, which means one rule to explain instead of two.
XP, capped weeklySay 100 XP a week. Playing helps but can never outpace real briefs. Honest, but it is a third rule, and caps need explaining to athletes who hit them.
XP, uncappedWhoever has the most free time wins the program. Not recommended, and the reason is above.
Coins, plus StandingCoins for winning, and regular play protects the Standing score. Keeps quiet athletes engaged without touching XP — and Standing already gates promotion, so it still carries weight.
If athletes can stake against each other

Stake coins, never XP. Coins are a balance; XP is a position on a ladder. An athlete who loses XP in a game can be demoted a Comfort Level by a game, which would make the ladder feel arbitrary to everyone watching. Whatever the game, the rule is the same: nothing a player does to another player may change their level.

Cohort boards when the cohort is small

Ranking inside a Comfort Level is the right call — it is what stops a new athlete being permanently invisible behind people two levels above them. But the levels are not the same size. Comfort Level - 5 will hold most of your roster; Zero Comfort has ten seats, three of them taken. A board of three is not a competition, it is a list.

  • Set a floor. Below about eight athletes in a level, merge upward with the next one and label the board honestly — Comfort Level - 1 & Zero Comfort.
  • Never merge downward from the top. Zero Comfort athletes merged into a Comfort Level - 3 board will sit permanently at the top of it, which is boring for them and demoralising for everyone else.
  • Say which pool they are in. The header on this screen reads Comfort Level - 1 · 18 athletes for exactly this reason. An athlete who cannot tell who they are being measured against will assume the worst.
